Ongoing assignments as of September 2026

Three of our current assignments, described in more detail.

Service 1 · Due diligence, appraisal and valuation

Full due diligence

Client
Kampani, Belgium
Subject
A proposed investment in an agro-industrial processor, Uganda
Our role
Consultant; one of more than 20 due diligence assignments for Kampani
Year
2026

Background

Kampani was considering an investment in a processor that had gone from several years of losses to a profit within a year of starting a new line of business. It needed to know how much of the turnaround would last and whether the numbers could be relied on.

What we did

The due diligence covered financial, governance, legal, technical and commercial matters, with a valuation and an investment appraisal. On the financial side we rebuilt five years of financial statements, traced the latest year's revenue to invoice-level records in URA's EFRIS system, recalculated the key ratios, checked the Board-approved budget line by line, and reconciled the asset register to the audited accounts and an independent valuation.

Result

The draft report showed that the year's profit depended on a few months of income from the new business and that the budget left out a material block of its own costs. It also raised questions on loan interest, tax and related parties. We recommended going ahead only on conditions: pricing based on adjusted earnings, conditions precedent, and protections in the contract.

Service 2 · Post-investment support

Loan monitoring

Client
Global Social Impact Fund (GSIF), Spain
Subject
A borrower, an agricultural commodity exporter, Uganda: one of more than 15 companies we monitor in GSIF's East African portfolio
Our role
Consultant
Status
Ongoing

Background

GSIF wanted an independent monthly view of a borrower that has loans from several development and impact lenders, a seasonal need for working capital, and a large capital project under way.

What we do

Each month we reconcile the total of the lenders' facilities to the borrowings on the balance sheet, compare current assets with working-capital borrowing, track capital drawdowns against construction in progress, and look at stock, customer concentration, commodity prices and exchange rates. Reports now end with a section on gaps in the data.

Result so far

The reports have shown that an improvement in liquidity came from new borrowing rather than from trading, flagged a sharp one-month fall in the funding left for capital spending, and pointed out that receivables were concentrated in a few customers. We also changed the method so that total loans now reconcile to the balance sheet.

Services 2 and 3 · Support, with M&E

Delinquency management

Client
aBi Finance Limited and 15 of its partner Tier IV financial institutions, Uganda
Our role
Lead consultant
Period
12 months from September 2026, in progress

Background

A significant share of aBi Finance's Tier IV partner institutions had portfolio at risk above industry norms. aBi Finance asked for a firm to work with 15 of them for 12 months to improve how they manage their loan books.

What we are doing

Loan arrears usually come from weaknesses at several points in the credit process, so the work covers the whole lending cycle. We set a baseline for each institution (PAR 1, 30 and 90, recovery rates, the quality of credit files and MIS reports), group the institutions by risk and maturity, adapt arrears ageing reports, PAR dashboards, early-warning checklists and board reporting formats to each one, and follow training with on-the-job coaching and review.

Status

Work started in September 2026. Results will be reported against each institution's baseline when the assignment closes.
